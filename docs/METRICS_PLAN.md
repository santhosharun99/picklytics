# Metrics plan (research workflow, 2026-10-03)

4 agents: industry/coaching metric scan (50 metrics: PB Vision, SwingVision, PPA stats, Tennis Abstract, coaching KPIs),
audit of our signals and accuracy (34 signals), ranked design, adversarial critique. Full output: workflow journal wf_0d779170-265.

## Build order (critic-revised)

1. Game-rule quality checks and depth correction for each end (small): check server stance, that serves stayed in play land in the diagonal box on the opposite half, that the serve and return bounce, and deuce/ad parity. Output: per-match depth offsets for each end, bounce recall for each source, and a gross-error list. Every depth metric waits on this. Evidence: far-side servers measure 4-5 ft behind the baseline (near 0-1.6 ft), and 6 serves land on the server's own half.
2. Trust layer v2 (small): per player and per end, measured vs eligible counts, including coverage on rally-ending shots; Wilson/bootstrap ranges and minimum-n display rules (hide % when n<10, grey when the CI is wider than 30 points); a video link to the shots behind every tile; export Event.score; remove out_pct and shots[].out; rename finishing/error shots to 'last detected shot'.
3. Time to reply plus 'takes the ball early' (now/small): median and IQR rather than the <0.8 s share, which is 55/233 on US but 3/214 on LV; drop pairs over about 3 s; split into hit-to-bounce and bounce-to-contact using the lifter's bounce frame; split by end.
4. Serve and return depth (small, after #1): separate for each receiving half with the depth offset applied; landings beyond the baseline on balls that stayed in play count as errors; serve, return and third shot identified from the server, not the raw index; return-depth win % with an explicit 'no landing / rally ended' bucket and ranges.
5. Pace bands (small): hard/mid/soft from ball_mph and lob from apex, with an 'unknown' bucket kept and no rough-speed fallback; rename speed_mph to ball_mph / rough_mph; rebuild the speed groups and third-shot mix on these bands; third-shot payoff only as hard vs soft vs unknown with ranges.
6. Forehand vs backhand in matched situations (small): returns, third shots, and baseline rally shots within one incoming-pace band; speed, depth and time left to the opponent, split by end, with coverage and ranges per wing.
7. One bounce truth (small-medium): the lifter's bounce decision replaces the volley flag and the mixed land_3d / landing_court; validate with the two-bounce rule and tags. This unlocks honest volley counts and consistent landing coverage.
8. Serve placement (small): deuce/ad straight from serve_x; 2 lateral bins (toward the sideline vs toward the centre); 'served to the backhand' from the return's measured stroke; filter gross errors with the box rule; counts per player, pooled across matches before showing %.
9. Directional tendencies (small): one ball-to-ball endpoint (lifter path[0] to path[-1]), mirrored for the end, shown as a 3x3 lane-by-direction table with a wing split; hand-check 40 shots across both ends before release.
10. Returner advance and first to the net (small, after #1): the returner's distance from the net at the third- and fourth-shot contacts, by third-shot pace band; first to the net and approach win % pooled across matches; replaces return-to-kitchen. Lateral opponent displacement goes with it, and depth-based versions follow once the end correction is checked.

## Critic issues (blocker / major)

- **[blocker] Serve depth bands + return depth (#3)**: Depth on the far half is biased long, and this shows on BOTH matches, not only LV. I checked outputs/*/analysis.json against the game rules. (1) A server must stand just behind the baseline, yet far-side servers measure 5.1 ft (US) and 4.1 ft (LV) behind it, against 0.0 and 1.6 ft for near-side servers. (2) Every serve in these rallies was returned, so it was in, yet 5 of 22 LV serves hit from the near end measure beyond the far baseline. (3) Median serve distance from the baseline depends on which end it was hit from: LV 0.8 ft from the near end vs 5.7 ft from the far end, US 2.8 vs 4.3. The plan's 3.2 / 2.6 ft medians mix the two halves. Serve counts are also lopsided (LV 32 vs 16, US 24 vs 40), so 'A serves deeper than B' mostly shows which end each player served from.  
  Fix: Report every depth stat separately for the near and far receiving half. Estimate a depth offset per half from the rules (server stance about 1 ft behind the baseline; balls that stayed in play land inside the lines) and apply it, or at least show it. Count landings beyond the baseline on balls that stayed in play as measurement errors, not as 'deep'. Lower reliability from high to medium until about 20 landings per end have been hand-clicked.
- **[blocker] Movement pack (first to net, return stance, recovery, opponent displacement depth, kitchen time, contact zones) (#10)**: The plan gates these on an LV-only check ('0% within 7 ft'), but the bias depends on which end the player is on and shows on US too. US contacts within 10 ft of the net: 34/154 from the near end vs 7/151 from the far end. LV: 7/140 vs 2/134. Far-player feet read about 3-4 ft too deep (see the server-stance check), so the far player looks like they never come forward. That decides 'who got to the net first'.  
  Fix: Use the same per-end depth offset as the serve stats (from server stance), then re-check about 20 hand-clicked feet on each end. Until then ship only lateral components (lateral displacement, lateral recovery) and hide anything that depends on depth.
- **[blocker] Return depth win %, third-shot payoff, response to pace**: Point-ending shots are almost never measured, so these stats leave out the shots that lose points. Shots with a speed: 24/64 (US) and 13/48 (LV) of rally-ending shots, against 215/241 and 208/226 of the others. Landing from the 3D lifter and net clearance: 0 rally-ending shots. A netted return or a failed counter therefore falls into 'unknown' and drops out of the buckets. Every 'win % after X' is inflated, and the error, which is the most important outcome, is invisible. This compounds the already small n (5-20 rallies per bucket).  
  Fix: Keep an explicit 'unmeasured / rally ended on this shot' bucket in every denominator and show its win %. Compute payoff from what is known for every shot (wing, server-relative shot number, hitter position, incoming pace). Frame response to pace from the hard ball's side: rally ended / soft / mid / hard / unknown. Show Wilson intervals. Only show win % differences whose ranges don't overlap, otherwise pool across matches.
- **[major] Points per service turn, serving runs, momentum runs (#9)**: This repeats an existing stat. Measured points per service turn equal won-on-serve p/(1-p): US 0.33 vs 0.33 and 1.33 vs 1.41; LV 1.54 vs 1.67 and 0.23 vs 0.23. The tile restates won_on_serve_pct, which the report already shows. Momentum runs over 50-60 rallies are mostly chance, and a single missed rally merges or splits them (LV is missing 5 of 53 points).  
  Fix: Drop it from the top 10. If wanted, show a per-game score-flow chart with the parity_ok / missed_points flags instead of run statistics.
- **[major] Pace and running trend across the match**: The plan says 'With a fixed camera the bias stays the same within a match'. That is wrong: players change ends between games (and at 6 in game 3), so the bias for each end turns into a fake fade or lift. The same US player's rally-shot speed changes by end (Devilliers 30.5 mph far vs 26.1 near, Boyer 36.9 far vs 43.5 near). With about 8 mph spread across shots, a median of 15 shots has a standard error of about 2.5 mph, so a fade of a few mph can't be detected.  
  Fix: Don't build it per match. If it's ever built, compare only stretches played from the same end, pool across matches, and show ranges.
- **[major] Serve placement by deuce/ad side (#6)**: The samples are too small and some data are badly wrong. Yoshitomi has only 8 serve landings in the LV report (out of 16 serves), against 32/32 for Fahey. Splitting deuce/ad by wide/body/T leaves about 1-2 serves per cell. 4 of 57 US and 2 of 40 LV serve landings normalise to the server's OWN half (e.g. (14.3,-23.8)), and these feed placement today. 'Body' isn't meaningful when the receiver stands deep and the ball bounces first. Mapping landing x to backhand through handedness piles one rule on another.  
  Fix: Use 2 lateral bins (toward the sideline vs toward the centre). Show counts, not %. Filter by rule: a serve must land in the diagonal box on the opposite half within ±2 ft, otherwise mark it 'measurement error' and investigate. Take 'served to the backhand' from the RETURN's measured stroke (89-97%). Read deuce/ad directly from serve_x, not from score parity, which lags when points are missed. Pool across matches before showing %.
- **[major] Measured shot family (pace/flight) (#4)**: (a) The fallback (feet-to-next-feet distance divided by hit-to-hit time) is the rough speed again. It underestimates speed off the paddle (drag, bounce, where the opponent stands), so fallback shots are pushed into lower bands. (b) There is a naming trap: report shots[].speed_mph IS ball_mph, while analysis.json shots[].speed_mph is the rough feet-to-bounce speed, the same mix-up that hurt the rule labels. (c) Only 'hard' can be checked against tags (42 drives). Soft rests on 6 tags. 34 of 129 US shots sit in 25-35 mph, where ±3-4 mph flips the band.  
  Fix: No fallback (leave 'unknown'), or a separate 'est.' fallback fitted on shots that have both speeds. Rename to ball_mph / rough_mph everywhere. Name the bands as pace (hard/mid/soft), not drive/drop. Show a speed histogram next to the shares. Validate soft/lob only after more tags.
- **[major] Forehand vs backhand quality comparison (#5)**: The comparison is skewed by shot choice and situation. Backhands are more often defensive blocks or resets, so a slower or shorter backhand can be a choice rather than a weakness. Stroke-rule errors cluster on balls hit in front of the body and wide balls on the far side, not at random. Each player plays different amounts from each end.  
  Fix: Compare wings within matched situations: returns only, third shots only, baseline rally shots within one incoming-pace band. Split by end, or check the near/far gap. Show coverage per wing and a bootstrap range for each difference.
- **[major] Time to reply (#2)**: (a) 'Share under 0.8 s' depends on the match: 55/233 pairs on US but 3/214 on LV, so it says nothing on baseline-heavy matches. (b) Outliers up to 5.84 s mean two hits were missed in a row. (c) The number mixes the hitter's pace and depth with the receiver's choice to take the ball early or let it drop deep, so it credits the hitter with pressure the receiver chose. Slicing by shot family inherits the family error.  
  Fix: Report median and IQR (or the match percentile) instead of a fixed threshold. Drop pairs over about 3 s. Split it into hit-to-bounce (hitter's pace and depth) and bounce-to-contact (receiver takes it early), using the lifter's bounce frame (0-1 frame off on tags). Split by end.
- **[major] Directional tendencies by hitter lane (#7)**: The endpoints are mixed: the landing point for balls that bounced, the opponent's contact point (lifter path[-1]) otherwise. The origin is the hitter's feet, which sit 2-4 ft from the ball on wide balls. Left/right flips with the end. Splitting by lane, wing and family gives tiny cells.  
  Fix: Use one ball-to-ball definition (lifter path[0] to path[-1] for every hit-to-hit shot), mirrored for the end. Show only a 3x3 lane-by-direction table, with wing as the one extra split. Hand-check about 40 shots across both ends before release.
- **[major] Coverage and trust badge (#1)**: n alone hides selection bias and gaps between players: Yoshitomi has serve landings on 8/16 serves, Fahey 32/32, and coverage is low on rally-ending shots. The existing low_sample flag (n<5) is far too loose: a win % on 5 rallies has a 95% CI of about ±40 points. Trust levels 'from PROGRESS.md' cover the signals, but most new metrics (direction, displacement, time to reply) have no validation, so everything would read 'experimental'.  
  Fix: Show coverage per player and per end, including coverage among rally-ending shots. Put Wilson or bootstrap ranges on every tile. Display rules: hide % when n<10, grey it out when the CI is wider than 30 points, and only say 'X > Y' when the ranges don't overlap. Tie each new metric to a 30-50 item hand check in label.html. Link every number to its shots and rallies in the video (the _stat rally ids already exist).
- **[major] Serve / return / third-shot identity (all opening-phase metrics)**: Shots are identified by raw index. A missed early hit that wasn't inferred shifts every label after it (shot-count error 0.29-0.78 per rally).  
  Fix: Define serve = first shot by the server (98% correct), return = the receiver's first shot, third = the server's second. Flag rallies where shot 0's hitter isn't the server, or where sides don't alternate, and leave them out of opening-phase stats.
- **[major] Landing depth by family / any landing stat**: land = land_3d or landing_court mixes two bounce sources that disagree on 98/227 US shots: 14 shots have only a lifter landing, 18 only a 2D one. Whether a landing exists at all is uncertain for a large share of shots, and the two sources have different errors.  
  Fix: Pick one bounce source (the lifter: 41/41 and 32/32 on tags), record which source each landing came from, and use the same decision for the volley flag.

## Missing ideas raised by the critic

- Use the game rules as free ground truth for each match: the server's feet must be just behind the baseline, a serve that stays in play must land in the diagonal box on the opposite half, the serve and the return must bounce (two-bounce rule), and deuce/ad alternates with score parity. These checks need no tagging. They give a quality badge per match, the bounce recall of each bounce source (US serves: lifter 52/57, 2D 53/57), and the depth offset for each end. Running them today found far-side servers 4-5 ft behind the baseline vs 0-1.6 ft on the near side, and 6 serves landing on the server's own half.
- Compare players with the court end taken into account. Within a game one player is near and one is far, so every player-vs-player stat (speed, depth, net approaches, coverage) should be split by end or corrected for it. US speeds for the same player differ by 4-7 mph between ends.
- 'Takes the ball early' (singles aggression): time from bounce to contact, and contact depth relative to the bounce point, using the lifter's bounce frame (0-1 frame off on tags) and hit timing. It needs no shot labels and splits time to reply into the hitter's part and the receiver's part.
- Returner advance: the returner's distance from the net at the third-shot and fourth-shot contacts (continuous). Also 'does the third shot keep the returner back': returner depth at the 4th shot by third-shot pace band. This is the core singles pattern and replaces return-to-kitchen (n=1-6).
- One bounce truth: let the lifter's bounce decision replace the event model's 'no bounce seen' volley flag, and use it for landings too. Validate it with the two-bounce rule and the 41/41 and 32/32 tags. This fixes L6 and the 98/227 disagreement, and unlocks volley counts and volley speed, which are currently on the do-not-build list.
- Video evidence for every number: each tile links to a playlist of the exact shots (timestamps already exist), so the coach can check the claim. Fixes the coach makes there are saved as tags that grow the validation set (currently 25 rallies).
- Rally-ending tags: add a one-click ending label (winner / forced error / unforced error / net / out) per rally in label.html. About 110 rallies takes roughly 20 minutes and unlocks the error and winner metrics the plan postpones.
- A player card across matches: pool each player over all processed matches, mark whether each tendency holds match by match, and show a percentile against everyone processed. A scouting tool is about the player, and pooling is the only real answer to 12-30 events per bucket.
- A small hand-checked set for each new metric (30-50 items in label.html: serve lateral bin, depth band, direction, approach, feet depth on each end) before the trust badge can say anything stronger than 'experimental'.
- A score-flow chart per game with the parity_ok / missed-points flags, as a visual replacement for the momentum and points-per-turn stats.

## Do not build yet

- **Serve/return in-play %, unreturned serve rate, net vs out fault split**: Pros miss only 1-3% of serves and returns, below our error: missed hits cluster on the last shot of a rally (hit detection 86-94%), short faulted rallies may not be segmented at all, and there are no ending tags. The net vs out split also needs in/out calls we can't make. Build after ending tags exist.
- **Unforced error rate/profile, winner-to-UE ratio, point-ending breakdown, rally aggression index**: rally_outcome is only about 50% right and unused. The point-ending shot has no 3D path or landing (68 US / 48 LV shots), and error type would rest on rule shot labels (65-70%) and unresolvable in/out calls. The existing 'finishing/error shots' already over-claim; label them as 'last detected shot' rather than extending them.
- **Any in/out-based stat (out %, line-hugging width %, deep-but-in)**: In/out was right on 1/5 US and 0/1 LV out tags, and within about 1.5 ft of a line one broadcast camera cannot resolve it. Also remove the hidden placement out_pct and analysis shots[].out from exported JSON so they aren't picked up later.
- **Volley / transition volley rate and volley speed**: The volley flag means 'no bounce seen'. The two bounce sources (event model vs lifter) disagree on 98/227 US and 42/223 LV shots, and 59% of LV volley flags were hit from more than 15 ft. Fix bounce/volley detection against tagged volleys first.
- **Dink tolerance, dink attackability, speed-up rate/success, counter-attack rate, attack height discipline**: Singles pros barely dink: the US match has 4 dink and about 30 reset/drop labels in total, all from 65-70% rule labels, so per-match counts would be in the single digits. Response to pace covers the singles version. Revisit for doubles or once contact height is validated.
- **Game-point conversion/save, clutch rating, shot selection by score**: There are about 10 late-game rallies per match on US and 1 on LV, where missed points made the score lag (9-0 vs a true 11-1). Needs several pooled matches plus the score trust flag, or manual score entry in the labelling tool.
- **In-attack share, conversion/steal, shot quality score, win-probability added (shot impact)**: These need an attack/neutral/defence state model or a rally-outcome model trained on hundreds of labelled rallies. We have 25 labelled rallies across 2 matches, so the output would look precise without being right.
- **Third-shot quality from 3D alone (opponent contact below net)**: The lifter's contact-end height has about 1.4 ft simulated error against a decision band of about 1.5 ft, and 10/227 US and 7/223 LV path ends are below the ground. Build it only through the pose-relative contact-height band, after a hand check.
- **Spin, paddle-face angle, serve legality**: Spin can't be measured from one broadcast camera. Paddle boxes are validated only as a hit clue (mAP50 0.81 on Roboflow), not for contact geometry.
- **Split-step / preparation timing, first-step acceleration, sprint top speed as a headline**: The swing probability is an internal hit clue with no technique ground truth. Feet are the bottom of the box at 10 Hz, too coarse for acceleration, and analysis.json top_speed_mph is impossible (27-47 mph). The filtered running_profile peak already covers the sane version.
- **Work-to-rest ratio and rally tempo**: Rest times are inflated by broadcast replays and rallies cut short when the ending goes to a side camera (median rest about 15 s vs rally 4.5-6.5 s). Low coaching value per match for the effort of cleaning it.
- **Doubles-only metrics (team shot share, poach/Erne/ATP, stacking) and return-to-kitchen in singles**: Current matches are singles. Return-to-kitchen has n=1-6 in singles and should be hidden there, replaced by first-to-net/approach payoff.

## All candidate metrics by theme (design agent)

### Serve and return (first two shots)
- **Serve depth bands** (now, reliability high, value high): For every serve, how far it lands from the receiver's baseline. Shown as the median distance in feet and the share landing within 3 ft, 3-7 ft and more than 7 ft from the baseline.
- **Serve placement by side** (small, reliability high, value high): Share of serves going to the wide, body and middle (T) thirds of the service box, split by deuce (right) and ad (left) side, plus the share landing on the receiver's backhand side.
- **Return depth and win % by return depth** (now, reliability medium, value high): How far each return lands from the server's baseline, in bands: deep (5 ft or less), mid (5-12 ft) and short (more than 12 ft). Also the returner's rally win % in each band.
- **Return stance and server hold-back** (now, reliability medium, value medium): Where the receiver stands when the serve is hit (feet behind the baseline, and lateral position), and how far the server is from the net when they hit the third shot.
### Shot families and the third shot (measured, not guessed)
- **Measured shot family (pace and flight)** (small, reliability medium, value high): Classify every rally shot by what the ball actually did, not by rules: hard (ball speed 38 mph or more), mid-pace (28-38 mph), soft (under 28 mph), lob (apex above about 10 ft), or unknown when not measured.
- **Third-shot family and payoff** (small, reliability medium, value high): For the serving player's third shot: the share that are hard, mid, soft or lob and forehand vs backhand, the rally win % after each, and how close the server got to the net by their next contact.
- **Opening-phase split (serve+1 / return+1)** (now, reliability medium, value medium): Share of points decided by the 4th shot vs later, and the player's win % in each, separately when serving and when returning.
### Pressure and pace (timing and 3D flight)
- **Time to reply (time pressure)** (now, reliability high, value high): Seconds from this player's contact to the opponent's next contact (the time they leave the opponent), and the reverse (the time they themselves get). Shown as a median and the share under 0.8 s, by wing, shot family and rally phase.
- **Response to pace (reset vs counter)** (small, reliability medium, value high): When the incoming ball is hard (38 mph or more), the share of replies that are soft (reset or block), mid or hard (counter), and the rally win % after each choice.
- **Net clearance and flight profile** (small, reliability medium, value medium): Median height over the net, the share within 1 ft (tight) and over 3 ft (floating), and the median apex, by shot family and wing.
- **Pace and running trend across the match** (now, reliability medium, value medium): Median hard-shot speed, serve speed and typical rally peak running speed per game (or per third of the match), early vs late.
### Placement and targeting
- **Directional tendencies by hitter lane** (small, reliability high, value high): For each shot, whether it goes cross-court, through the middle or down the line, based on the hitter's lane (left, middle or right third) and where the ball arrives. Split by lane, wing and shot family.
- **Forehand vs backhand quality comparison** (small, reliability high, value high): Forehand and backhand side by side: share of shots, median speed, landing depth, net clearance and the time left to the opponent.
- **Landing depth by shot family** (small, reliability medium, value medium): Median landing distance from the opponent's baseline and the share deep (7 ft or less), mid (7-15 ft) and short (more than 15 ft, near the kitchen), for each measured shot family.
- **Opponent displacement (who is doing the running)** (small, reliability medium, value high): How far the opponent had to move between this player's contact and their own, from where they stood at your contact to where they hit. Shown as a median in feet and the share of shots forcing more than 6 ft, with both players compared and lateral and total distance reported separately.
### Court position and the singles net game
- **First to the net and approach payoff** (small, reliability medium, value high): In each rally: whether each player got within about 10 ft of the net, on which shot, and who got there first. Also the rally win % for the player who came in, and the family, depth and direction of the shot they approached behind.
- **Passing situations** (small, reliability low, value high): When the opponent is within about 10 ft of the net at this player's contact: the share of passes down the line, cross-court and lobs, and the rally win % after each.
- **Recovery position at opponent contact** (small, reliability medium, value medium): Where this player stands when the opponent hits: lateral offset from the covering position (court centre adjusted toward the opponent's lane) and depth, plus the share of opponent contacts where they are more than about 5 ft off.
### Contact height
- **Contact height bands (own and forced)** (medium, reliability low, value high): Ball height at each contact relative to the hitter's body (below knee, knee to waist, waist to shoulder, above shoulder), both for the player's own contacts and for the contacts they force on the opponent.
### Score, serve turns and momentum
- **Points per service turn and serving runs** (now, reliability medium, value medium): Points scored per service turn, the share of service turns that score at least one point, and the longest serving run per game.
- **Momentum runs and run-stopping** (now, reliability medium, value medium): Each player's longest run of rallies won and of points scored, and how many rallies it took to stop an opponent run of 3 or more.
- **Win % by score situation** (small, reliability low, value medium): Rally win % when leading, level or trailing, and in the late game (either player on 7 or more), split by serving and returning.
### Trust layer
- **Coverage and trust badge on every stat** (small, reliability high, value high): For every stat, show how many eligible shots or rallies were actually measured (for example 'serve depth: 57 of 65 serves') and a trust level: validated, plausible or experimental.
